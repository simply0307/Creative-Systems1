Rumin Timeline: The Jewel of Rumie
1. The Founding of Rumie

Rumie begins with the brothers Rolmus and Remex. Together, they found the city, but their partnership fractures over the question that will define Rumin history: should Rumie be built through trade or conquest?

Rolmus represents the mercantile dream of Rumie, believing trade can build empires. Remex argues that trade survives only behind walls. Their conflict establishes Rumie as a civilization born from the tension between commerce, security, expansion, and domination.

2. The Fragile Republic

Generations later, Rumie grows wealthy under the Republic. Its senators, banks, runes, and legions make the city powerful, but corruption spreads through the institutions meant to preserve it.

The Republic endures because it is slow, procedural, and legalistic, but that same slowness makes it vulnerable to purchase. Corrupt officials and aristocrats begin shaping Rumie into a city where wealth can bend public life.

3. The Rise of Kaiser

Kaiser emerges as a beloved officer who walks among workers, pays debts, and exposes a corrupt governor protected by the aristocracy. He becomes a hero of the people by attacking corruption directly.

This marks the beginning of Kaiser’s legend. He is not yet emperor, but he becomes the figure ordinary Rumins trust more than the slow Republic.

4. The Gaulic Wars

Northern tribes unite against Rumie under the Gaulic chieftain Vercan. Kaiser turns the frontier war into fame, wealth, and open trade routes.

This is the moment Kaiser transforms from popular officer into imperial conqueror. Rumin roads expand outward, and military victory becomes economic infrastructure.

5. The Discovery of the Three Runes

Kaiser discovers sacred vaults containing the runes of Strength, Protection, and Experience. He begins binding these runes to the legions.

The Rumin military is no longer merely disciplined and well-funded. It becomes metaphysically enhanced. Sacred power enters the machinery of empire.

6. The First Empire Bank

Kaiser returns to Rumie and builds roads, grain systems, public works, and banking reforms. Saboteurs try to break Rumie’s markets, but Kaiser centralizes credit and public survival around his reforms.

This event establishes banking as one of Rumin’s central engines. Rumie eats because credit moves, but the danger is clear: if one man commands the credit, one man also answers for starvation.

7. The Crossing

The Senate orders Kaiser to surrender command. Brutus pleads with him not to march, but Kaiser refuses, arguing that surrender would return Rumie to corruption.

Kaiser crosses the point of no return and civil war begins. This event marks the death spiral of the old Republic.

8. The Last Republic

Rumie burns as legions and senators collide. Kaiser wins the city, but Brutus survives the fall of the old order.

Kaiser believes he has saved Rumie from the men who sold it. Brutus believes Kaiser has saved Rumie by conquering it. Both men understand that the Republic has been transformed beyond recovery.

9. Emperor of Gold

At Kaiser’s peak, roads, banks, and legions flourish. Rumie is prosperous, but prisoners, taxes, and central rule make citizens question the jewel.

The empire becomes materially successful but politically compromised. Rumin prosperity now exists beside obedience, captivity, and centralized power.

10. The Ides of Rumie

Kaiser stabilizes the empire, but conspirators led by Brutus reach the Senate floor. Kaiser is assassinated.

The tragedy is not framed as a simple triumph of liberty. Brutus kills the tyrant, but Kaiser claims Brutus acts because Rumie no longer needs him. The assassination becomes both betrayal and transition.

11. The War of the Successors

After Kaiser dies, Bobei seeks vengeance while Brutus tries to restore the Republic from the ruins.

The old Republic cannot simply return. Kaiser’s death wakes an empire, and Rumie mourns the man Brutus calls a tyrant.

12. The First Emperor

Augustus defeats Bobei the Great. He keeps Kaiser’s bank, legions, and rune program while restoring Senate traditions.

Rumie ends this era as an empire wearing republican robes. The forms of the Republic survive, but imperial power becomes the true structure underneath them.
