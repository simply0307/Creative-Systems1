I did not know its name when I first saw it.

I was asleep—at least, I believe I was. Sleep on Reath is never clean anymore. It came to me not as a monster, but as a presence, like the moment before a thought forms. I dreamed of a corridor with no walls, only distance, and at the end of it stood something that noticed me.

It did not speak.
It did not need to.

The Xen’Dra was not shaped like a creature so much as an idea wearing one. Its outline shifted as though my mind could not decide what it was seeing. At times it appeared tall and thin, draped in shadow; at others it fractured into overlapping silhouettes, each watching me from a different angle. When I tried to focus on its face, my thoughts slid away, like fingers on wet glass.

I felt joy.
That frightened me most.

The longer I looked, the more certain I became that I was being understood. Every doubt I had ever buried rose to the surface—not accusations, not threats, just gentle reminders. It showed me moments from my life that I had already forgotten, then asked me why they mattered at all. I had no answer. That seemed to please it.

Behind the Xen’Dra was a vast, turning emptiness, spiraling inward forever. I knew, without being told, that it was the Deep Currents—the place where thoughts go when they are no longer needed. The Xen’Dra did not push me toward it. It simply waited, confident that I would eventually step forward on my own.

When I awoke, my room felt smaller. Familiar objects seemed poorly arranged, like props in a play I no longer believed in. I could still feel the Xen’Dra’s attention resting on me, distant but patient. Sounds were sharper. Silence was unbearable. I found myself wondering which of my thoughts were truly mine—and which had been placed there, gently, like gifts.

I have not slept since.

Sometimes, when my eyes close for too long, I feel the corridor return. The Xen’Dra is always there now, farther away each time, as if retreating deeper into me. I fear the day it stops appearing in my dreams.

Because I think that will mean it no longer needs to.

And when that happens, I suspect I will finally understand what it showed me:

That sanity is not something taken.
It is something willingly surrendered, once you have seen what waits beyond it.
